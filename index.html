Executive Summary

Investment thesis

  • Scale with a cross-sell engine. AUM compounded at 26.8% over FY22–26 to ₹5,09,975 cr while the customer franchise doubled to 119.33 mn. AUM per customer rose 25% to ₹42.7k and 63% of customers are already in the cross-sell franchise — growth is coming from deeper wallets, not just new names.
  • Margin give-up is being bought back through efficiency. NIM eased 87 bps (FY23–26) to 9.73% as the book tilted to secured assets (63% of AUM), but cost-to-income fell to 33.3% and opex/AUM to 3.84%, so pre-provision profit still grew 22% in FY26.
  • Credit cost has peaked. FY25–26 credit cost of 2.2%/2.09% absorbed MSME stress and a ₹1,406 cr LGD-floor ECL top-up. The model normalises it to 1.52% in FY27E (guidance 1.45–1.60%); H1 FY27 provisions are at 49% of the full-year estimate.
  • Earnings re-accelerate and are tracking. Model PAT compounds at 24.9% (FY26–30E) to ₹47,121 cr with ROE rebuilding to 20.9%–21.8%. H1 FY27 PAT of ₹12,448 cr (+28% YoY) already equals 47% of FY27E.
  • Valuation: upside on multiples, rich on intrinsic. At ₹957 the stock trades at 22.9x FY27E EPS and 4.39x FY27E BVPS versus target multiples of 25x / 5.5x → target ₹1,122 (+17.2%). The Residual Income value of ₹725 implies the market already prices a long high-ROE runway — the key debate for this BUY.

01Company Overview & Profile

Bajaj Finance Ltd. (BFL) is India’s largest private-sector NBFC, a deposit-taking upper-layer NBFC regulated by the RBI and the lending flagship of the Bajaj Group. Born in 1987 as a captive two-wheeler financier, it now runs 13 lending lines — consumer, personal, MSME, commercial, rural, gold, auto, securities-backed and mortgages (through 86.70%-owned Bajaj Housing Finance) — alongside deposits, payments, insurance distribution and broking. Its model is “acquire and cross-sell”: the franchise serves 1 in 3 Indian households, booked 52.45 mn loans in FY2026 and crossed ₹5 lakh crore of AUM. The FY2026–30 Long Range Strategy targets 200–220 mn customers and a 19–21% ROE by FY2030 through AI-led operations (“FINAI”) and a lowest-risk underwriting posture.

02Industry & Competitive Landscape

NBFCs complement banks by reaching customers and geographies conventional channels under-serve; NBFC credit rose from 8.9% of GDP in FY2015 to 14.6% in FY2025. The macro backdrop is supportive — real GDP grew 7.6% in FY2026 with private consumption at ~56% of GDP — and benign inflation let the RBI cut the repo rate from 6.50% to 5.25% (Dec-24 to Dec-25), a lagged tailwind for wholesale funding costs. Regulation scales with size: under Scale-Based Regulation BFL is an upper-layer NBFC, and the RBI’s May 2025 digital-lending directions raise conduct standards for app-led credit.

Competitive landscape & BFL position

BFL competes with diversified NBFCs (Shriram Finance, Cholamandalam, L&T Finance, Tata Capital), gold lenders (Muthoot Finance), private banks in personal loans and mortgages, and fintechs in small-ticket credit. Its edge is a rare combination: AAA-rated liabilities including ₹68,533 cr of public deposits, an average cost of funds of 7.54%, behavioural data on 119 mn customers, and product breadth that lets it pivot growth toward secured assets without losing scale.

INDUSTRY OPPORTUNITIESGrowth drivers

Credit deepening — NBFC credit/GDP at 14.6% and rising; ~40% of BFL’s new customers are new-to-credit.

Rate cycle — 125 bps of repo cuts reprice borrowings with a lag (exit CoF 7.41% vs 7.54% average).

Secured adjacencies — gold (+115%), CV & tractor (+136%), LAS (+31%) and car loans (+26%) in FY26.

BFL POSITIONKey strengths

Franchise — 119.3 mn customers, 63% in the cross-sell pool, 6.07 products per customer.

Funding — AAA/Stable from CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, Ind-Ra; S&P BBB, Moody’s Baa3; LCR 128%.

Risk & capital — GNPA 1.01%, NNPA 0.41%, PCR 60%, CRAR 21.55%.

KEY RISKSIndustry challenges

Unsecured stress cycles — BFL’s MSME GNPA rose to 2.65% in FY26 from 1.48%.

Regulatory tightening — risk weights on consumer credit, digital-lending and NBFC-UL norms.

Yield competition — banks and fintechs compress spreads in prime segments.

06DuPont Analysis (ROA Tree) & Common-Size

For a lender, debt is raw material, so the generic DuPont (margin × asset turnover × leverage) becomes the banking ROA tree: each P&L line as a % of average loans (AUF) — the BFSI common-size statement — with ROE = ROA × equity multiplier. It reconciles exactly to reported ROA (FY26: 4.26%).

DuPont summary

  • ROA fell from 5.31% (FY23) to 4.26% (FY26): NIM compression (-87 bps) and higher credit cost (+68 bps) were only partly offset by lower opex (+76 bps benefit).
